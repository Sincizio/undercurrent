Undercurrent — Report Specifications

Version 0.3 — 2026-11-10. Defines the four products. The Weekly is the MVP; Flash and Monthly are specified now so that ledgers and storylines are built to serve them from day one.

0. Common rules (all products)

  • Language: English. Plain, precise, analytic. No hype, no clickbait, no partisan framing. Sentences carry information, not atmosphere.
  • Data before judgement: within every section, facts (with notes) precede interpretation; interpretation is visibly marked (“Reading:”, “Hypothesis:”).
  • Notes: numbered per section, placed at the end of the section, in the format of 02-methodology §11. Conflicts between sources are stated in the note.
  • Labels: claims of type leak/rumor/sentiment/forecast are always labelled inline, e.g. [rumor, 35%], [sentiment], [forecast, 70%, by 2027-03].
  • Probability words follow the standard table (02-methodology §8.1).
  • Operational hypotheses: short form at the end of a section where pertinent; structured form in the dedicated summary section. Granularity always stated.
  • Header: product name, issue number, date, period covered, estimated reading time.
  • Footer: methodology link, scorecard link, disclaimer (standard text in §5), subscription/archive links.
  • Formatting for email: HTML generated from Markdown with inline styles; tables rendered as HTML tables (the email must survive copy-paste into mail clients).

1. Weekly (MVP)

Delivery: Sunday 07:00 Europe/Rome. Period: the seven days ending on the Saturday before publication (Sunday 00:00 to Saturday 24:00, Europe/Rome). The email is held until 06:45 and delivered at about 07:00. Length: 2–5 pages (≈1,800–3,500 words excluding notes). Reading time target: 12–20 minutes.

Structure

1. Highlights (5–8 bullets) The week’s most material developments across all domains, one line each, each tagged with domain and materiality band (High / Medium) and a pointer to its section. At most one bullet may be a weak signal, explicitly labelled.

2. The Week in One Map (one paragraph, ≤150 words) How the week’s developments interact across domains: the connective reading that no single-domain feed provides. Written last, placed second.

3. Domain sections — six sections, depth proportional to the Relevance Register. Together they take about 60% of the issue (≈1,100–2,100 words); the other 40% belongs to the cross-cutting sections, where the integration work lives. Domain sections appear in order of weight. Indicative word budget at the current weights (0.29 / 0.21 / 0.18 / 0.14 / 0.11 / 0.07): Economy & Markets 300–600 · Technology 225–450 · Geopolitics 200–375 · Science, Medicine & Ecology 150–300 · National politics 120–230 · Culture & Sport 75–150. Cross-cutting sections (≈700–1,400 words): Highlights 100–180 · Map ≤150 · Weak signals 200–350 · Chains 200–400 · Forecasts and watchlist 100–220 · Scorecard ≤100; the hypotheses table and the notes are not counted. Each section follows the same internal pattern:

  • What happened — facts with notes, ordered by materiality.
  • Reading — why it matters, what it changes, what remains uncertain.
  • Hypothesis (where pertinent) — one to three short operational hypotheses with granularity, direction, confidence, invalidation trigger.
  • Notes. Culture & Sport is treated for its economic and social signal value (rights, attendance, sponsorship, soft power, cultural shifts), not as entertainment coverage.

4. Undercurrent — weak signals (3–7 items) From the Rumor Ledger: each with claim, origin tier and community type (not necessarily the named account), detection method, probability, resolution criterion and date, linkage if true. Updates on previously published signals (confirmed / falsified / still open), with lead time where confirmed.

5. Chains (2–4 chains) Chains of effects of the week in the fixed format (02-methodology §5.4): hops with mechanism, confidence and horizon; end-node implication at stated granularity; falsifier. Mix of synchronous and asynchronous chains when available.

6. Forecasts and watchlist

  • New forecasts (statement, probability, date, criterion, reasoning).
  • Updates to open forecasts (previous → new value, trigger).
  • Week ahead: dated events and data releases that matter for open storylines and hypotheses.

7. Operational hypotheses — summary table All hypotheses of the issue in one table: thesis · granularity · direction · horizon · confidence · invalidation trigger · watch. Grouped by granularity (market → sector → asset class → single name).

8. Scorecard (brief) Rumors and forecasts resolved this week, with outcome and one-line lesson. Running calibration summary (count, Brier to date) once enough resolutions exist.

9. Method note and disclaimer (standard text, §5).

Style register

The Weekly ranges from terse chronicle (in What happened) to developed analytical prose (in Reading, Map and Chains). Variation of register is deliberate: facts fast, thinking slow.

2. Flash (Phase 3)

Delivery: Monday–Saturday 07:00 Europe/Rome. Length: 15–20 lines. Content: only items with materiality ≥ 0.6 or status changes in ledgers (a rumor confirmed, a forecast resolved, a hypothesis invalidated). Structure:

  • 3–6 priority items, one or two lines each, labelled and noted (notes as compact inline source tags).
  • Undercurrent: 0–2 new weak signals, one line each.
  • Today: 1–3 dated events to watch. No analysis beyond one clause per item; the Flash points to the Weekly and to storylines for context. If nothing clears the threshold, the Flash says so in three lines rather than inventing priority.

3. Monthly (Phase 4)

Delivery: first Sunday of the month, 07:00, alongside (not replacing) the Weekly. Length: 2–5 pages. Nature: pure elaboration and integration; no chronicle. Structure:

  1. State of the world in five theses — the engine’s current reading of the structural picture, each thesis with confidence and what would change it.
  2. Scenario analysis — 2–3 storylines developed into branching scenarios with probabilities, indicators and implications by granularity.
  3. Verified effects review — for last month’s high-materiality items: did the expected effects occur? Evidence, lessons, resulting relevance adjustments.
  4. Calibration report — Brier by domain and horizon; reliability table; notable hits and misses; rumor radar hit rate by method and community type; lead-time distribution.
  5. Revisions — theses, forecasts and hypotheses retired or changed, with reasons.
  6. Vertical deep dive — one topic treated at depth (technology, sector, region, mechanism), with its own chains and hypotheses.
  7. Relevance Register changes — topic weights moved, with justification.
  8. Operational hypotheses — monthly view — longer-horizon positioning table.
  9. Method note and disclaimer.

4. Spot (on demand)

Requested by the owner (later by readers through a defined channel). A single-topic analysis using the same engine: claims, sources, chains, forecasts, hypotheses, QA. Length as needed. Archived alongside periodic issues and linked from the relevant storyline.

5. Standard texts

Disclaimer (footer of every issue):

Undercurrent is independent analysis for general information. Operational hypotheses express the authors’ reading of public information at the date of publication, with the stated probabilities and invalidation triggers; they are not personalised investment, legal or medical advice and do not consider any reader’s circumstances. Sources, methodology and the full record of past calls are public.

Method note (footer, one line):

How we weigh sources, publish weak signals and score our forecasts: [methodology link]. Scorecard: [link].

6. Archive conventions

  • File naming: reports/weekly/YYYY-Www.md, reports/flash/YYYY-MM-DD.md, reports/monthly/YYYY-MM.md, reports/spot/YYYY-MM-DD-slug.md.
  • Each report carries front matter: product, issue number, date, period, word count, QA status, ledger version used.
  • The site renders an index per product, a search over all issues, a storylines page, a scorecard page and the methodology.
