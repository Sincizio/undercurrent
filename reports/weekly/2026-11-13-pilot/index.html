The Week in One Map

Reading: Three abrupt developments moved large markets. A company lost its chief executive days before its results. A president closed a negotiation with a post, and oil gave back part of its peace discount within two sessions. And the bond market tightened ahead of the Fed: yields above 5% are already visible in the weakest home sales since 2010. The links run one way. Dearer oil keeps inflation up; inflation keeps a December rate rise alive; and the cost of money presses on households and on the financing of the AI build-out. Diplomacy widened, with a European seat on Ukraine and a possible three-leader meeting at APEC, while Moscow’s territorial position did not move. Climate-finance pledges shrank as warnings about a very strong El Niño continued.

Economy & Markets

What happened

  • The 10-year Treasury yield rose above 5.05% on 12 November, a three-week high; MarketWatch described bond yields as seeing their biggest surge since 2022. [1]
  • The Federal Reserve published a revised tentative meeting schedule for 2027. CNBC reports that rates will be voted on at seven meetings and that October’s will be a discussion session with no vote and no statement. [2]
  • Brent rose to about $96 a barrel on 12 November from a ten-week low of $91.5 two days earlier, after the president’s statement on Iran. [3]
  • Saudi Arabia reported a 26% rise in October crude output and Iraq a wartime record, while exports still depend on alternative routes and military protection, according to one data aggregator. [4]
  • US existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October to a 3.75 million annual rate, 8.8% below a year earlier and the lowest since August 2010; the 30-year mortgage rate averaged 7.33%, up from 6.86% in September. [5]
  • The UK economy grew 0.7% in the three months to September, its fastest rate for more than two years. [6]
  • Nvidia shares closed down 4.34% on 12 November, with more than 400 billion lost since the start of the week; the S&P 500 held up, MarketWatch reported. [7]
  • AI-infrastructure results diverged on 12 November: Nebius fell 16% on higher 2026 capital spending and a weak outlook, Cisco beat estimates and said its AI infrastructure revenue will double this fiscal year, and Cerebras rose 17%. [8]
  • Anthropic’s listing terms, per a specialist IPO researcher: 825 million shares at $70–78, with a $26bn private placement to seven technology companies including Nvidia and TSMC; pricing is expected in the week of 16 November. [9]

Reading: Two days undid much of last week’s relief. Crude had fallen on hopes of a settlement with Iran; when Washington closed the negotiating track, Brent recovered about $4.5. The bond market did not wait for the Fed. One aggregator puts the market-implied probability of a December rise at about 91% [sentiment], a single-source figure that we could not check against futures data. [1] Housing is where tighter money is already visible, before any December decision. Britain’s 0.7% growth makes it harder for the Bank of England to look through energy prices. The equity market’s response to Nvidia was narrow. Unresolved: what the Fed’s schedule change means, since we saw only the title of its release; whether Gulf supply is recovering as fast as one source says; and where Brent settled.

Hypothesis: long-duration US and UK government bonds stay exposed — market · underweight · months · confidence 0.60 (plausible; was 0.55) · invalidation trigger: 10-year Treasury yield closes below 4.60%, or the December FOMC holds and drops its tightening bias.

Technology

What happened

  • Nvidia announced on 12 November that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director with immediate effect. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim chief executive and keeps her post; director Harvey C. Jones becomes the company’s first board chair; the search for a successor covers internal and external candidates. The release gives no reason. Results remain due on 17 November. [1]
  • Singapore’s home affairs ministry barred former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang from re-entering the country, saying she had given police inaccurate information about her role at the company. [2]
  • TSMC reported October revenue of about NT$586.89bn, up 59.7% from a year earlier and 14.7% from September. [3]
  • TSMC is to invest $1bn in Anthropic and OpenAI, Ars Technica reported. [4]
  • A Microsoft 365 outage on 12 November drew nearly 130,000 user reports; the company blamed a server problem. [5]
  • US nonprofits sued large technology companies in California, asking for a freeze on training “catastrophic” AI models. [6]
  • Maersk is investigating data theft from what it believes is a legacy system; the German military grounded Quantum Systems drones after a cyberattack on their maker; Yandex said drone attacks on its data centres caused data loss. [7]

Reading: Last week the question was whether Nvidia’s board would keep its chief executive. It now has to find one while its own inquiry, Congress’s and, by Al Jazeera’s account, the FBI’s continue. [8] Reports attributed to the New York Times and Reuters say that a further workplace relationship came to light on 11 November and that the board met that evening [leak, 70%]; the company has given no reason. [9] A separate unnamed-source report says senior White House officials are discussing the commerce secretary’s future over Chinese entity listings and Nvidia’s exports [leak, 50%]. [10] If both hold, the affair has moved from one company’s governance to pressure on export-control policy. We found no bill text or vote, so our forecast on chip provisions is unchanged. TSMC’s October revenue shows no weakness, but monthly revenue lags orders and does not test last week’s signal that Nvidia is slowing them [rumor, 30%].

Hypothesis: for Nvidia, governance and legislation drive the range of outcomes more than demand — single name · hedge · weeks · confidence 0.65 (plausible; was 0.60) · invalidation trigger (reworded): a permanent chief executive is named and the defence bill passes without chip provisions.

Hypothesis: US political risk to data-centre siting is structural — sector · watch · months · confidence 0.45 (uncertain; unchanged) · invalidation trigger: Texas lifts its permit restrictions in the 2027 session or federal law pre-empts local limits.

Geopolitics

What happened

  • President Trump wrote on 12 November that the United States does not need a deal with Iran, said Washington holds Iran’s money and “their Strait”, and directed Vice-President Vance to work on domestic anti-fraud efforts instead of the negotiations. [1]
  • Iranian officials had hardened their own line: a senior security official warned that any base used in a new US war would be a target, the parliament speaker said the Strait of Hormuz will not return to its pre-war status, and Tehran rejected a proposal to move its 60%-enriched uranium abroad. [2]
  • The trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi ended on 11 November without a breakthrough but with agreement on a prisoner exchange. [3]
  • In Moscow the next day, President Putin told US envoys Witkoff and Kushner that he would not soften his demand for full control of four Ukrainian regions and that Russia would target any foreign troops sent to Ukraine. [4]
  • Mr Witkoff said Mr Putin had agreed to European negotiators joining the next round; President Zelensky, after meeting the envoys in Dublin, said Europeans will be there. EU governments have not settled who would go. [5]
  • Secretary of State Rubio and China’s foreign minister discussed a possible three-way Trump–Xi–Putin meeting at next week’s APEC summit in Shenzhen, according to the State Department. [6]
  • An Israeli air strike on Nasser hospital in Gaza killed six people including a surgeon, according to the hospital’s director; the military said it had targeted a Hamas commander there. [7]
  • Two Israeli party leaders agreed on a candidate for Knesset speaker, pending other leaders’ approval, but not on which parties join the government. [8]

Reading: Last issue’s de-escalation case rested on statements, and this week a statement reversed it. Washington now presents its leverage as a substitute for an agreement, and Tehran’s speaker describes Hormuz as permanently changed. We found no report of new strikes on Iran in the material we hold. An Israeli broadcaster reported that the caretaker security cabinet ordered preparations for an attack on Iran [leak, 50%]; we could not read the original. [9] On Ukraine the process widened while the territorial position stated in Moscow did not move. Sources differ on how firm the European seat is. We could not verify Iran’s response to the 12 November statement, the state of Hormuz traffic, or any Saudi–Houthi event on 11–12 November.

Science, Medicine & Ecology

What happened

  • COP31’s leaders’ summit opened on 11 November with the British prime minister the only G7 leader present; the United States was absent for a second year. [1]
  • A Carbon Brief analysis finds that annualised public climate-finance pledges by 23 developed countries and the EU fall from about $40bn a year, under pledges that mostly ran to 2025, to under $12bn a year for the rest of the decade, a drop of about 70%; the United States has made no pledge. [2]
  • The host’s voluntary electrification pledge (35% by 2035) still had only scattered backing; the EU is reported to support it if the electricity is “clean”. [3]
  • Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to being declared free of the virus. [4]
  • A study of 844,108 adults published in Nature found that a framework for diagnosing obesity that combines body-mass index with waist measures and organ function outperforms criteria based on BMI alone. [5]
  • Forecasters warned of heavy rain and flooding in Kenya from an El Niño that could be one of the strongest on record. [6]

Reading: The summit’s attendance and the finance figures point the same way: the countries that funded the last decade’s pledges are not renewing them at scale. The figures are announced pledges, not money delivered. The Guardian understands that China has ruled out hosting COP33 in 2028 [leak, 60%]. [7] On El Niño, official confirmation is partial. NOAA’s status of 26 October, before our period, keeps an advisory in force and calls a strong-to-very-strong event most likely through January–March 2027; we could not find the monthly update expected on 12 November. [8] The obesity study is peer-reviewed; we saw the journal’s summaries, not the paper.
